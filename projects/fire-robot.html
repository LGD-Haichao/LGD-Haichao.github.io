A fire-warning robot that moves toward the risk

A two-person team project: a SparkLink HiSpark car with multispectral flame sensing and AI vision for mall fire warning

Fixed detectors wait for the fire to come to them. A mall is bigger than a detector's reach.

Context

Conventional fire alarms are cheap and reliable in a single room, but public spaces have blind spots: a corridor turns, a storefront is closed, a kitchen fire starts behind a wall. Fixed sensors also struggle with steam, dust, and cooking smoke, which is why nuisance alarms are common. A mobile platform can move toward the risk instead of waiting for it.

The project

With Wang Yuqian, I built a two-person entry for the 8th National Undergraduate Embedded Chip and System Design Competition. The platform was a SparkLink (星闪) HiSpark car carrying a multispectral flame sensor and a camera. The car patrolled a mapped route, looked for the spectral signature of a flame, and used an AI image model to confirm the detection before triggering an alarm. The control loop was: sense → decide → move → confirm → report.

My role

I was the technical lead on a two-person team. I worked on the AI image-recognition pipeline, the multispectral sensing logic, and the closed-loop integration. The project plan lists me as the technical lead; the competition certificate lists both team members.

How it works

  • Mobility: a HiSpark car with a defined patrol route and manual override.
  • Multispectral flame sensing: a flame sensor array that looks at characteristic wavelengths rather than simple brightness, reducing false positives from lamps and sunlight.
  • AI vision: a lightweight image classifier confirms the presence and rough location of a flame before the alarm is raised.
  • Distributed control: HarmonyOS distributed capabilities connect the car, the sensor board, and the alarm endpoint.
  • Response: local sound/light alarm plus a remote notification path.

Outcomes

  • 8th National Undergraduate Embedded Chip and System Design Competition, Chip Application Track — North Region 3rd Prize (2025).
  • Certificate no. SOC-01022529472.
  • A working prototype and a complete competition demonstration.

What I learned

Two people and a deadline is a useful constraint. We had to choose one failure mode to solve well — false alarms — and accept a simpler patrol model. I also learned that a demo is a product argument: the judges remember the moment the car turns toward the flame, not the slide that lists the sensors.
