Short-term teaching is easy to do badly. We tried to do it carefully.

Context

Qingdui, a historic town on the northern coast of the Bohai Sea, has the texture of a place that once mattered: Ming and Qing merchant houses, a local drum tradition, narrow streets that remember a busier harbor. It also has the education problems common to rural northeast China — aging teachers, limited extracurricular offerings, and a high share of children whose parents work elsewhere.

Our 15-person team from the Ganlu Charity Association spent 14 days there in July 2025. We were not a tutoring squad. The plan was to combine subject teaching with cultural research, parent conversations, and a public session, and to leave something the local partners could keep using.

My role

I was the deputy team lead. I co-led the team with the team lead, was responsible for part of the activity organization and discipline, handled parent and teacher communication, and taught all high-school chemistry lessons plus selected math, physics, and primary-school classes. I also wrote all 13 program articles and supported filming.

How I worked

Build a curriculum, not a timetable

We designed four modules — mental health and emotional management, aerospace history as a way into scientific thinking, everyday safety and emergency response, and functional fitness — and paired each with a simple teaching kit. The modules were meant to be usable by a teacher who had never met us.

Treat parents as stakeholders

Conversations with parents were not a courtesy. They were how we learned which children were caring for younger siblings, which families were considering moving away, and which students were quiet because they were bored rather than shy. That changed how we grouped classes and how we wrote the final report.

Document as you go

I wrote an article every day, usually after the evening meeting. Thirteen pieces is not a marketing number; it was the only way to keep the record honest while the days blurred together. The articles became the raw material for the research report.

Outcomes

  • 15 members, 14 days, four curriculum modules, parent conversations, and a public session with the local care-for-youth committee.
  • University 2nd Prize for social practice; Research Report 1st Prize; personally named Outstanding Individual 2nd Prize.
  • Program covered by the Dalian Municipal Working Committee for the Care of the Next Generation and Zhuanghe TV.
  • 13 articles published on the team's WeChat account.

What I learned

Teaching is translation. The chemistry was not the hard part; the hard part was deciding which idea was worth 40 minutes and which analogy would survive a classroom with one fan and thirty chairs. I also learned that a deputy's job is mostly removing ambiguity — who is where, who is responsible, and what happens if the plan breaks.

Credits

Team led by Song Yi; faculty advisors Zhang Tianyu and Zhu Xiaoxuan. The curriculum and teaching were shared; I was responsible for discipline, parent communication, chemistry teaching, and the written record.
